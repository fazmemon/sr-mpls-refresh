Where we all started: gold, silver, bronze

Three classes. Mark the traffic, give the gold queue priority at every box, hope the rest sorts itself out.

gold
priority queue
voice, control traffic, the CEO's video
silver
weighted share
business applications
bronze
whatever is left
bulk, backups, the internet
  • It worked, mostly, because the links were slow and the classes were few. Priority at each hop was enough to keep the small important flow ahead of the big one.
  • What it promised was a treatment at each box. What it never promised was an outcome for the application at the far end.

1 · 2

Then DiffServ: the same idea, standardised

What it gave us
  • A code point in every packet, and a per-hop behaviour for each: expedited forwarding, four assured classes with drop precedence, best effort.
  • Trust boundaries, policers at the edge, a scheduler per box. Every vendor implements it; every fabric runs some of it today, PFC and ECN included.
What it still did not give us
  • The unit is the hop. A per-hop behaviour says how a box treats a class. It says nothing about what the class gets end to end.
  • Loss and delay are handled as two separate knobs: a queue depth here, a drop profile there. The job experiences them as one thing.

RFC 2474 and RFC 2475, the DiffServ architecture

1 · 4

The kicker: it does not add up

Three ways the sum fails
  • Percentiles do not add. p99 at the leaf plus p99 at the spine is not p99 of the path. Neither is mean plus mean the mean the job feels when the queues fill together.
  • Loss is not in the budget. A hop that holds its 10 µs by dropping has met its number and broken the job. The retransmit is the delay nobody budgeted.
  • The budget was set on averages. Each share was sized from a utilisation figure or an idle ping. The moments that matter were never in the data.
  • Adding tails assumes the hops are independent. In a fabric they are not: a collective fills the leaf queue and the spine queue in the same microsecond, so the per-hop sum under-protects. Where hops really are independent the sum over-protects, which is where the 64× comes from. Either way it is not the path's tail.
What would make it add up
  • A quantity per hop that combines into the same quantity for the path, by a known rule.
  • One that carries loss and delay together, because that is what the job feels.
  • One that can be measured on the live path, cheaply, and contracted at a boundary.
  • That quantity exists. It is called quality attenuation, written ΔQ, and it is standardised. The rest of this chapter is why we need it; chapter 2 is what it is.

Link utilisation does not tell us if the job is fine

One minute on a 100G link: what the packets see, and what the counter reports
Illustration, synthetic trace. The effect it shows is documented: sub-second bursts four times or more above the averaged peak.
  • Our counters are sampled every 30 s to 5 minutes. A microburst lasts microseconds. The buffer fills and drops inside a single sample, and the sample says 40 %.
  • Scale of the gap: ten minutes on a gigabit link holds as many packets as two years of cars on a three-lane motorway. On 100G, multiply by a hundred. The motorway averages "quiet" and still jams every evening.
  • For an average to mean anything here you would need it over a few hundred microseconds. Nobody keeps that. The silicon can timestamp at that resolution; we do not read it.

The "bandwidth" a 10 kB request needs, depending on how fast you want it back
  • Same request, same bytes. Want it back in 3 s and it "needs" 29 kbit/s. Accept 8 s and it needs 11. The bandwidth figure is an output of the quality delivered, not an input.
  • That is why a capacity plan written in Gbit/s cannot be handed from the fabric team to the DCI team to the provider. Each adds its own margin, because none of them can say what the others' figure means for the job.

So every team adds a margin, and the margins multiply

Capacity bought as a multiple of the need, when each team protects itself with a factor of two
  • NIC, leaf, spine, DCI, provider, far end. Six teams between a job and its data is not unusual. Each one, unable to see the others' headroom, keeps its own.
  • At 2⁶ the estate holds 64 times the need and still cannot say whether the collective will finish on time.
  • Most of that capacity is bought to cover what are really queueing and scheduling problems. That is the money this deck is about.

Chapter 2

What ΔQ is

One measure, built from what can be observed. Four properties carry the whole method.

Property 1
Quality can only be lost
Every element attenuates. Nothing adds quality back.
Property 2
Delay and loss are one distribution
Loss is the part of the curve that never arrives.
Property 3
It is conserved
Along a path it only grows. At a queue it can only be shared out.
Property 4
It composes
The path is the sum of its hops. So a requirement becomes a budget.

2 · 2

Delay and loss are one distribution

ΔQ as a curve: the chance a packet has arrived by time t
Illustrative distribution. The shape is the point: the curve never reaches 1, and the gap is the loss.
  • A distribution built from a sample of packets, or the odds for the next one. Both views are the same object.
  • It is an improper random variable: its total probability is less than one. The missing mass is the probability of never arriving, which is how loss is carried without a second metric.
  • "Packets are not people." It is the odds that matter, not the fate of any one packet. The system is memoryless; it does not owe a flow anything for the last drop.
  • Everything in the definition is observable, which is what lets it be measured, contracted and standardised.

PNSol workshop pp.48–49 · TR-452.1 §2.3 and §7.2 · Davies & Thompson, IETF 2021, §3

2 · 3

The game of chance at every queue

A packet arriving at a finite queue finds it in one of three states. That is the whole game, played once per hop.

EMPTY
Served at once
Additional attenuation close to zero. The packet lands on the lower boundary of the delay plot.
IN BETWEEN
Waits behind the others
Delay depends on how many are ahead. This is the variable, contention part.
FULL
Lost
Infinite delay, as far as the endpoints can tell. Loss is a value judgement they make on a packet that took too long.
  • The network's state is unknown at the moment a packet is submitted. Hence a game of chance, with odds that shift with load.
  • Networks are trees of multiplexers. Given routing, the elements a flow crosses are fixed; only the other flows sharing those elements change its odds. The complexity is bounded.
  • Loss is not a fault to fear. It is a necessary part of stability and consistent performance, and a lever to be used deliberately.

PNSol workshop pp.3, 49–50 · TR-452.1 §7.1.2

2 · 4

ΔQ is conserved

"Probably the most important point of the day." Delay cannot be undone and a lost packet stays lost. Along a path the attenuation only accumulates. At a scheduling point, the total is fixed and can only be shared out.

The thought experiment
  • Take a fixed set of packets holding a fixed amount of data. The time to send them all is the same whatever order you send them in. Total delay is conserved.
  • You can reduce total delay by deliberately losing some packets. That trades delay for loss. The attenuation is conserved.
  • So no scheduling algorithm "does work". It apportions impairment. Its worth is judged by outcomes across the flows, never at the queue.
Three things people sell as fixes, which are trades
  • De-jittering buffers the early and drops the late: more delay and more loss. Done at every hop of a VoIP path it is "really daft, but endemic".
  • Circuit emulation over IP needs 5 to 7.5 ms of buffer each way for TDM-grade loss: 10 to 15 ms round trip, "moving the radio access network to the mid-Atlantic ridge".
  • Forward error correction buys lower loss with capacity. A trade, chosen for the outcome, not a free lunch.

PNSol workshop pp.4–5, 51–52 · Davies, Ofcom 2009, slide 13

2 · 5

It composes along the path

Two hops, each with its own delay distribution, and the path they make together
Illustrative hop distributions; the path curve is their actual convolution, computed on the page.
  • For independent hops the path's ΔQ is the convolution of the hops' ΔQs. Written ΔQA→Z = ΔQA→B ⊕ ΔQB→C ⊕ … The loss multiplies through the same operation.
  • Composition is stronger than abstraction: it lets a requirement at the top be split into budgets at every hop and for every supplier, and it works the other way, from measured hops to a predicted path.
  • This is the property bandwidth lacks, and the reason a bandwidth SLA breaks down across a supply chain.
  • Convolution assumes independent hops. So measure the path as well as the hops. The gap between the composed prediction and the measured path is the correlation, and it is itself a diagnostic: it says the queues are filling together.

TR-452.1 §7.2.1, equation (3) · IETF 2021 §3.3 · PNSol workshop pp.7, 54, 93

Three parts: G, S and V

One-way delay against packet size. Sorted by size, the structure appears.
Points simulated from the published ADSL figures (G 16.9 ms, S 13.3 µs per octet, V mean 1.7 ms). The line is the fit through the minima.
  • G, geographic. The intercept: a zero-length packet's transit. Speed of light, lookups, media access. Set by design.
  • S, serialisation. The slope: time per byte. Set by link rate. Every "faster" link in history has been a smaller S.
  • V, variable contention. The scatter above the line: time spent behind other traffic. Set by scheduling. The only one you manage.
  • "Jitter" conflates S and V, so it manages the wrong thing. Even an absolutely prioritised packet still sees some V.

2 · 8

Two degrees of freedom, so loss and delay are traded

Every finite queue
  • Three quantities: offered load, loss, delay. Fix two and the third is fixed. Fix one and you fix a relationship between the other two.
  • At fixed load, less loss means more delay. At fixed loss, more load means more delay. You never get to choose all three.
  • A lossless fabric is this trade made by policy. PFC turns every would-be drop into waiting, so loss comes back as delay, head-of-line blocking and pause storms. ECN marks are the same trade signalled early. ΔQ puts what that policy costs the tail on one curve.
  • Leave the trade unmanaged and you get an arbitrary relationship between loss and delay as load creeps up, then a capacity purchase. The cycle repeats.

Why the boundaries
  • Because ΔQ is conserved, a path measured "good enough" end to end means no sub-path introduced excessive attenuation.
  • If it is not good enough, one extra observation point says which half. Each added point halves the search: localisation scales as log₂ of the path.
What the probes record
  • Four timed events per hop: reception starts and ends, transmission starts and ends. Three of the four suffice if the interface rate and packet size are known.
  • Enough to identify each packet: stream id, size, a short sequence number or a hash. Never the payload.
  • The root cause is often not in the management domain where the impairment showed. Measurement across boundaries is what makes that visible.

From timestamps to G, S and V in six steps

1
Match the packets
The same packet at each observation point, each direction. Infer loss from the ones that never match.
2
Correct the clocks
Systematic offset between points. The sum of G both ways is stationary whatever the skew; the analysis uses that.
3
Sort by size
For each size, the minimum delay is the one that saw empty queues everywhere.
4
Fit a line through the minima
Linear regression. Holds even when S is stepped by cell or frame quantisation.
5
Read G and S
Intercept is G. Slope is S. Together, the structural delay of the path.
6
What remains is V
Subtract the line. V's distribution, by time and by segment, is where contention shows.
  • Two assumptions: larger packets take at least as long on average, and some packets of each size see negligible contention. Both hold in practice.
  • Then V against time says when, and V per segment says where. In fixed networks G and S are stable, so a continuous measurement compresses to almost nothing.

3 · 5

Configuring to the contracts

Once the QTAs through an element are known, the scheduling it needs can be computed. Contracts compose horizontally along the path and vertically at a multiplexing point, so this scales.

The levers
  • Classes by cherish and urgency. A flow's cell in the trading grid is its treatment: which loss it may take, which delay.
  • An economy class. To run an element hot, something must absorb the attenuation that the standard and superior classes shed. Give it a deliberately weak contract so its attenuation never triggers an upgrade. Time-shiftable work belongs there.
  • Pre-contention at ingress. Know the contention points and shape before them, so the queues that matter never fill. This is how the first quality-assured ISP made sign-language video work over 256 kbit/s DSL.
  • Capacity, last. Only once the structure says G or S is the limit.
What to know before you touch a scheduler
  • The structural budget, G and S, of the existing path. That is how much attenuation is left over for multiplexing.
  • That V's stability depends on the number of flows, not only their rate.
  • That a priority class's gain is paid by the rest, with worse variance. Conservation.
  • That the rate of reconfiguration is low and the computation is cheap. Faster reconfiguration without feedback on the same timescale only arms new hazards.

PNSol workshop pp.36, 111–115 · Geddes, "How Wales got the first Internet fast lane" (2015)

a curve, each direction, with the failure allowance in it
  • No outcome can be promised without a constraint on demand. The constraint need not be onerous, but it must exist. Today's IP contracts promise open-ended quality for unbounded load: "a particular section of purgatory".
  • The contract also says what happens beyond it: does the flow prefer more delay or more loss when it exceeds its load; does the supply have a burst mode, for how long; what the alarm or penalty is when the curve is breached; and when the contract may be suspended, as in load shedding or a civil emergency.
  • It is quantitative, measurable, and in principle enforceable: the same rigour a legal process needs, and the basis for detecting quality fraud.

From aspiration to assurance, and assurance done beforehand

ASPIRATION
What users want
Measurable, may be too high or infeasible at the price. Negotiable. Not contractable as it stands.
REQUIREMENT
Statistical bounds per flow
Loss and delay, instantaneous. Measurable, so adherence can be assured.
EXECUTION
Supply meets demand
At the shortest timescale. Model and predict the configuration; do not "put it together and see". Make things fail in the right order.
ASSURANCE
Did we deliver, at each stage?
Today: open loop, post-mortem, complaint counts. Here: measure how far overbooking has armed the hazards before any matures.
  • Assurance against a predictive model discriminates when and what to upgrade. "The cost savings are staggering."
  • Three integration levels for any system: build to work, build to test, build to operate in the field. The hazards that reach the field are the ones no one modelled; some only appear at scale, in space or in time.

  • Elastic and inelastic flows need different treatment. RPC and low-duty-cycle flows inside long-lived TCP sessions are inelastic: TCP is being used for assured delivery there, not for rate adaptation.

Feasibility before the build

Where the method came from
  • A safety-of-life programme: a battlefield network stood up in 48 hours, under attack, with a required order of failure ("voice is the last to go").
  • Systems engineers from aerospace could not get a performance case from network engineers who reasoned hop by hop in bandwidth. The calculus was built to give them one.
  • It showed some use cases could not be met on the infrastructure being built. The programme was later cancelled with a tenth of its budget spent.
What it looks like now
  • Outcome diagrams: the observable outcomes a system must deliver, with ΔQ bounds, independent of its internal structure. Performance bounds as distributions over all possible executions, before detailed design.
  • The same approach has been used for a globally distributed blockchain's block diffusion, avionics, and telecoms.
  • Two categories of project it catches early: infeasible (cannot deliver the quality at any price) and uneconomic (the revenue cannot cover the cost of the quality). "Tens of billions" sit in the second.

Geddes, FCS series (2015) · Haeri, Thompson, Davies et al., "Mind Your Outcomes", Computers 2022 · PNSol workshop p.89

locate
Add a point, halve. Expect the cause to sit in a different domain from the symptom.
which lever
G: design and topology. S: link rate. V: scheduling. Most fabric tails are V. With PFC there are no drops: the packets that would have been dropped wait instead, so that loss shows up as extra queueing delay, which is V.
contract and budget
Write the requirement as a curve with a failure allowance. Devolve it across the hops and the suppliers. A slice or a pipe with a strong SLA is a place to multiplex a contract into.
run hot
An economy class for the bulk. Both directions bounded for the elastic flows. Watch the burst-loss mechanisms. Buy capacity last, and only where the structure says so.
