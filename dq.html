There is no quality in averages

One minute on a link: what the packets see, and what the counter reports
Illustration of the effect described in TR-452.1 §4.2.2. Synthetic trace; the bursts run about four times the reported average.
  • Counters sampled every 1 to 15 minutes smooth away sub-second bursts that run four times or more above the averaged peak.
  • Ten minutes on a gigabit link holds as many events as two years of traffic on a three-lane motorway. The motorway averages "quiet" over two years and still gridlocks every evening.
  • For an average to mean anything on that link you would need it over 20 to 250 ms. Nobody collects that.

  • Both outlined cells happened on the same network in the same week. The first published comparison of utilisation with a direct quality measure.

Bandwidth is an average, and it does not compose

not the same thing
1 Mbit/s on an empty link ≠ the last 1 Mbit/s on a loaded one
not the same thing
1/10 of a 100 Mbit/s port ≠ a 10 Mbit/s port
not the same thing
two 100 M links in series ≠ one 100 M link
The "bandwidth" a 10 kB transfer needs is set by the quality delivered to it
  • Same data, same transfer. Want it in 3 s, you "need" 29 kbit/s; accept 8 s, 11 kbit/s. The requirement is a consequence of delivered quality, not a cause.
  • So a budget written in bandwidth cannot be handed down a supply chain. Each party adds its own margin instead.

What the safety margins add up to

Capacity bought as a multiple of the need, when each layer protects itself with a factor of two
  • Each silo cannot see the others' margins, so it adds its own. The margins multiply.
  • Six layers is not unusual between a user and a service. At 2⁶ the estate holds 64 times the need, and still cannot say whether the application will work.
  • Capacity is being bought to cover what are, rightly, scheduling problems. That is the misallocation this deck is about.

The large estates already say this

The Internet Architecture Board's 2021 workshop on measuring network quality for end users wrote these down as consensus.

"Bandwidth is necessary but not alone sufficient."RFC 9318, general statement 1
"Latency mean and medians are distractions from better measurements."RFC 9318, problem statement 1
"A really meaningful metric for users is whether their application will work properly or fail because of a lack of a network with sufficient characteristics."RFC 9318, general statement 5
  • The operating language of the big estates is already the tail: 99th percentiles, latency under load, responsiveness.
  • What that language lacks is a measure that is both a network measure and an outcome measure, and that composes along a path. That is the gap ΔQ fills.

RFC 9318, "IAB Workshop Report: Measuring Network Quality for End-Users", October 2022, §5

Chapter 2

What ΔQ is

  • Quality is the absence of attenuation. Every element only ever loses and delays.
  • One improper distribution: delay on the curve, loss as the mass that never arrives.
  • Conserved: it only ever grows along a path; at a queue, scheduling can only distribute it.
  • Composes: the path's ΔQ is the convolution of its hops. Budgets follow.
  • G, S and V: the design, the link rate, the contention. Only V is yours to schedule.
  • Two degrees of freedom per queue: fix the load and you trade loss for delay.
  • A requirement is a stepped curve with a failure allowance; slack is the gap to what you deliver.

Chapter 3

How to set up

  • Measurement: observation points, the same packets at two or more places, each direction.
  • A valid test stream: five or more sizes across a 10:1 range, randomly spaced, a few thousand samples.
  • Plot delay against size: intercept G, slope S, scatter V. Then V by time and by segment.
  • The standard: TR-452.1. Carried over STAMP/TWAMP. Clocks need not be synchronised across domains.
  • Configuration: scheduling derived from the contracts through an element; the cherish/urgency grid; an economy class so the fabric can run hot; pre-contention at ingress.
