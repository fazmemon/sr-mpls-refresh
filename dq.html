Chapter 1

Why the way we reason today fails

  • An application only ever sees loss and delay. Everything else is hidden by layering.
  • The numbers we manage by, bandwidth, utilisation, average latency, are averages. Averages hide the events that break applications.
  • Bandwidth is not fungible and does not compose. A budget written in bandwidth cannot be handed down a supply chain.
  • The large estates already measure the tail. What they lack is a measure that composes.

1 · 1

The application sees one thing: loss and delay

  • The great achievement of layering: the application has one thing to worry about with respect to the network, the impairment its packet streams experience.
  • A connectivity failure, whatever the cause, is just unbounded delay.
  • "Insufficient bandwidth" really means: at this offered load, the loss and delay exceeded what the application can take.

TR-452.1 §4.1–4.2; PNSol workshop notes p.58

1 · 2

Loss and delay together decide the outcome

  • Capacity is not the limit here; the server is instantly fast. Time to complete is purely a function of the transport's loss and delay and the protocol's behaviour.
  • A lost packet shows up to the user as more delay, so loss and delay sit on the same contour.
  • At the 95th percentile the same transfer runs 5.5 s to 21 s, and which packets were lost starts to matter: the handshake packets most.

PNSol workshop pp.60–61; TR-452.1 Figures 4 and 5

1 · 3

There is no quality in averages

  • Counters sampled every 1 to 15 minutes smooth away sub-second bursts that run four times or more above the averaged peak.
  • Ten minutes on a gigabit link holds as many events as two years of traffic on a three-lane motorway. The motorway can average "quiet" over two years and still gridlock every evening.
  • For an average to mean anything on that link you would need it over 20 to 250 ms. Nobody collects that.

TR-452.1 §4.2.2 (micro-bursts); PNSol workshop notes p.69

1 · 4

Utilisation does not predict whether voice works

Kent Public Service Network, a well-run public-sector network, 1,300 sites. Five days of data, each link's utilisation beside a direct voice-quality breach metric (5 ms at 99.5 %).

  • Links at 80 % busy carrying voice perfectly: evenly spaced packets fit between.
  • Links at under 1 % busy degrading voice: the traffic came in bursts.
  • The first published comparison of utilisation with a direct quality measure. Common across engagements since.

An SLA written in averages passes while the service is dead.

  • A monthly-average loss target of 0.1 % is met by a 40-minute outage once a month.
  • "We won't support voice unless load is under 90 %" is a rule of thumb that is wrong in both directions.
Their verdict: "The average metrics don't give you the data you need to manage cost and performance." They ran the network hotter and deferred a £1M upgrade.

PNSol "no quality in averages" deck slides 35–37; KPSN interview (Aldington, 2015); "high-fidelity measures" deck slides 8–9

1 · 5

Bandwidth is an average, and it does not compose

  • The same figure of merit hides different loss and delay, so it says nothing about whether the application works.
  • Two pieces of "pipe" in series are not one pipe. "Bandwidth" wrapped across a supply chain cannot be handed down as a budget, so each party adds its own safety margin.
  • Where it was learned: a safety-of-life programme whose network engineers reasoned hop by hop in bandwidth could not say whether the system would work. It could not.

TR-452.1 §4.2.2 and §6.3; PNSol workshop pp.122–123; Geddes, "Five key lessons from Future Combat Systems" (2015)

1 · 6

What the safety margins add up to

  • Each silo cannot see the others' margins, so it adds its own. The margins multiply.
  • Capacity is being bought to cover what are, rightly, scheduling problems. That is the misallocation the whole deck is about.

PNSol workshop notes p.127; "The Ladder" deck slides 108–113

1 · 7

The large estates already say this

The Internet Architecture Board's 2021 workshop on measuring network quality for end users wrote down, as consensus:

"Bandwidth is necessary but not alone sufficient."
"Latency mean and medians are distractions from better measurements."
"A really meaningful metric for users is whether their application will work properly or fail because of a lack of a network with sufficient characteristics."
  • The operating language of the big estates is already the tail: 99th percentiles, latency under load, responsiveness.
  • What that language lacks is a measure that is both a network measure and an outcome measure, and that composes along a path so it can be budgeted. That is the gap ΔQ fills.

RFC 9318, "IAB Workshop Report: Measuring Network Quality for End-Users" (Oct 2022), §5.1 and §5.3

1 · 8

Idle latency is not the latency anyone experiences

  • Speed tests and idle pings measure the propagation floor. Under load the same path is 6 to 25 times slower, and that is what the application sees.
  • The estates that run at scale manage to the loaded tail. This deck gives that practice a measure that composes.

RFC 9318 §4.2.4

1 · 9

What this deck claims

  • One measure. Delay and loss in a single distribution, for a path, in each direction.
  • It composes. The path's measure is the sum of its parts, so a requirement at the top becomes a budget at every hop and for every supplier.
  • It is cheap to get. A low-rate test stream, a few thousand samples, timestamps at two or more points. Standardised in 2020.
  • It tells you which lever. The measure splits into what the design did, what the link rate did, and what contention did. Only the last is yours to schedule.
Chapter 2 defines the measure. Chapter 3 shows how to get it and configure to it. Chapter 4 shows how to contract, budget and assure with it.

Chapter 2

What ΔQ is

  • Quality is the absence of attenuation. Every element only ever loses and delays.
  • One improper distribution: delay on the curve, loss as the mass that never arrives.
  • Conserved: it only ever grows along a path; at a queue, scheduling can only distribute it.
  • Composes: the path's ΔQ is the convolution of its hops. Budgets follow.
  • G, S and V: the design, the link rate, the contention. Only V is yours to schedule.
  • Two degrees of freedom per queue: fix the load and you trade loss for delay.
  • A requirement is a stepped curve with a failure allowance; slack is the gap to what you deliver.

Chapter 3

How to set up

  • Measurement: observation points, the same packets at two or more places, each direction.
  • A valid test stream: five or more sizes across a 10:1 range, randomly spaced, a few thousand samples.
  • Plot delay against size: intercept G, slope S, scatter V. Then V by time and by segment.
  • The standard: TR-452.1. Carried over STAMP/TWAMP. Clocks need not be synchronised across domains.
  • Configuration: scheduling derived from the contracts through an element; the cherish/urgency grid; an economy class so the fabric can run hot; pre-contention at ingress.
